AI + HIGH-PERFORMANCE COMPUTING

Teaching transformers to read DNA

Fine-tuning DNABERT and a from-scratch Transformer to classify regulatory DNA from ENCODE, packaged as a bootcamp curriculum with Slurm job generation and distributed training on Perlmutter.

The problem

Scientific machine learning brings together concepts that are often taught separately: biological data, tokenization, neural networks, and the practical work of running jobs on a supercomputer. This project made that full workflow accessible within a 10–12-hour bootcamp project.

What I built

I developed and tested DNA sequence-classification workflows using PyTorch, Hugging Face, and DNABERT-style transformers on NERSC Perlmutter and ALCF Polaris and Aurora. The work covered CPU baselines, GPU-accelerated training, and fixed-configuration experiments to isolate acceleration benefits.

I created Jupyter lessons and diagrams explaining embeddings, transformer architectures, and classification pipelines, alongside SLURM workflows using sbatch, squeue, and sacct.

A smaller sequence representation

I compared 6-mer and byte-pair encoding (BPE) tokenization. BPE used substantially fewer tokens per sequence while achieving similar accuracy in the comparison.

Leading the project team

As project lead, I coached a 10-student team within the 87-student bootcamp. Two sub-teams took different approaches and presented their findings to peers and laboratory staff.

  • One sub-team trained a DNA transformer from scratch, compared five tokenizations, and achieved a validation AUROC of 0.887 using overlapping 6-mers.
  • The second fine-tuned DNABERT to classify CTCF binding on K562 data, achieving 91.7% evaluation accuracy and 99.1% training accuracy after about 17 minutes of training. The work also included external testing on an astrocyte CTCF dataset.

These are results from the separate student experiments I guided; the tokenization comparison above is a distinct experiment.

Making the work reusable

I authored a first-time project-lead guide and a reusable project template to help future interns scope a project, support a team, and prepare technical presentations.

Explore the curriculum

The public repository includes the two learning tracks, notebook pre-flight checks, ENCODE data provenance, and job-generation workflows for distributed training on Perlmutter. Every cluster path and the Slurm account are read from the environment, so another project can run the curriculum without editing a notebook.
